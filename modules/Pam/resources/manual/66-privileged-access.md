---
title: Privileged access
audience: agents
---

**Privileged access** keeps the accounts that can do the most damage, and their passwords and keys, in one protected place. It stores credentials. It does not connect to your systems, change passwords on them or record sessions.

## Getting a credential

Open an account that was granted to you. Give a reason and a code from your authenticator app, then **Show the credential**. It hides itself after a minute. Each showing is recorded with your reason, and the people who review access can read it. If you only use a passkey or your company's sign-in, set up an authenticator app first in your account's security page. A newly set up app can show credentials only after 24 hours, and you get an email when one is set up: if you did not do it, tell your administrator at once.

If you do not see the account, or it says you have no access, ask its owner or whoever manages the vault.

## Keeping accounts

Whoever manages the vault, and each account's owner, can edit an account, replace its credential and choose who may ask for it. Replacing a credential, giving access and deleting an account also ask for a code from your authenticator app. When access is given, the person, the owner and other managers get an email. Managing does not mean reading: to see a credential you need access like anyone else, and another person has to give it to you, because nobody can grant themselves access.

Replacing the credential only updates what is stored here. Change it on the system itself as well.

Deleting an account destroys its stored credential for good. Its history stays.

## Reviewing use

**Access log** lists who was shown which credential, when and why, for those with the right to read it, such as security officers and auditors.
