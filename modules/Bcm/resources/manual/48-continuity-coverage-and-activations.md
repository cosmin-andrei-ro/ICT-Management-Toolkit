## Continuity coverage

**Risk & continuity → Continuity coverage** shows, at a glance, whether the business is protected:

- the **figures** at the top: assessed processes without a plan, plans not approved, reviews and exercises overdue, versions waiting for approval, exercise findings not handed to IT support;
- the **plans**: version in force, next review, last exercise and its outcome, next exercise due, and the estimated recovery against the strictest RTO of what the plan covers — in red when the plan would not bring the business back in time;
- the **assessed business processes**: the plans in force that cover each one, whether one brings it back within its RTO, and whether it was exercised. Tick **Only what needs attention** to keep the gaps.

**Export to spreadsheet** prepares the continuity register — plans, process coverage, exercises, findings and activations — for auditors. It appears in the list above the figures when ready.

## Reminders

A plan is due for review on the date set at approval, and for an exercise twelve months after the last one (or after its approval if it was never exercised). About a month before, its owner and deputy get an email, once. A review already started, or an exercise already planned, counts as done.

Every Monday, continuity coordinators get an email listing what needs chasing, when there is anything.

## Activating a plan

When a real disruption happens and the plan is activated, open the plan and click **Activate the plan**:

1. Enter when it was activated, the scenario and what happened.
2. Leave **Email the crisis team** ticked to send the crisis team members who have an account, and the plan's owner and deputy, a link to the plan. The email does not contain the plan itself.
3. **Record the activation.**

Raise the incident in Ticketing as usual: the activation records the continuity side.

While the crisis lasts, the activation page links to the printable plan. When the service is back, enter **Service back at**, how it went and the lessons learned; the page compares the time to recover with the strictest RTO. **Close the activation** once everything is written down: it is then kept as a record and can no longer be changed.

The plan's owner and deputy, the continuity coordinator, and team leads for IT recovery plans can activate a plan in force; everyone who can see the plan can see its activations.
