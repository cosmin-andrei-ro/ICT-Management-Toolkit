---
title: Projects
audience: everyone
---

**Projects** lists the IT projects you can see. Project managers and team leads see every project; others see the projects they sponsor, run or work on. **My tasks** gathers your unfinished tasks from every open project, the most urgent first.

## Starting a project

Project managers and team leads click **New project** and give:

- a **name** and a **description**;
- the **type**: *New application* when the project brings an application into the company, *Infrastructure*, or *General*;
- the **project manager** and the **sponsor**;
- the planned **start** and **end** dates.

The project gets a code (PRJ-0001) and four phases: **initiation**, **planning**, **execution** and **closure**. It starts in initiation. The project manager plans each phase's dates with **Plan dates**.

## The team

Under **Team**, the project manager adds people with their role in the project, such as "Network engineer" or "Key user". Someone who still has open tasks cannot be removed; give their tasks to someone else first.

## Tasks

The project manager adds tasks under **Add a task**, with:

- the **phase** it belongs to;
- who it is **assigned to**: the manager, the sponsor or a team member;
- a **due date**.

Tasks show as a **Board** with four columns, *To do*, *In progress*, *Blocked* and *Done*, or as a **List**. Filter them by phase. The buttons on each card move it to the next or previous column. The project manager moves any task; each person moves the tasks assigned to them, from the project or from **My tasks**. Overdue tasks are marked in red.

## Deliverables

Under **Deliverables**, the project manager lists what each phase produces, such as "Go-live change", "Recovery plan" or "Training". Each deliverable is either **required to close** or optional. Then:

- **Link a record** connects a deliverable to what it became in another module: choose the kind of record and search it. Only records you may see are offered.
- **Mark delivered** records who delivered it and when.

## New applications

A project of type *New application* has a **checklist**. First choose the application in the portfolio that the project brings in. The checklist then shows, as they become true:

1. the application is recorded in the portfolio;
2. its documentation is approved;
3. it has its item in the CMDB, created when the documentation is approved;
4. at least one of its risks is assessed in the risk register;
5. the business processes it serves, linked in its documentation, are assessed in the BIA with their RTO.

Nothing is ticked by hand: each item comes from its module.

## Moving from one phase to the next

A project moves on only when its **sponsor** agrees. Under **Stage gate**, the project manager writes what the sponsor should know and clicks **Ask to move to** the next phase. In the closure phase, the button is **Ask to close the project**.

The sponsor is emailed and finds the request on the project and under **My tasks**. They then:

- **Approve**: the phase is done and the next one starts, or the project is closed;
- or **Send back**, with a comment saying why.

The manager is emailed either way. The sponsor never decides on their own request, so a project needs a sponsor other than its manager.

A project closes only when its required deliverables are delivered and, for a new application, its checklist is complete. Until then, the page lists what is missing, and both asking to close and approving the closure are refused.

## Status reports

From time to time, the project manager clicks **Write a status report**. They rate the project **green**, **amber** or **red**, overall and for schedule, budget and scope, then write:

- what was done since the last report;
- the next steps;
- any issues and risks.

The page keeps every report. A report older than two weeks is due again, and the manager gets a reminder on Monday.

**Portfolio** (from the Projects page, for project managers and the roles that read every project) shows each open project with:

- its phase and its latest status, with the date;
- any gate waiting for its sponsor;
- its manager and sponsor, and its planned end.

## Closed projects

Closed and cancelled projects stay as they ended and can no longer be changed.
