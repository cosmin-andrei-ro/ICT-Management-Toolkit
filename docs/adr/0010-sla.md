# ADR 0010 — SLA clocks in working time

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-11-03

## Context

Tickets must be answered and resolved within targets that depend on their priority, counted in working time (office hours, without weekends and public holidays). Agents need warnings before a target is missed, and reports need to know whether each target was met.

## Decision

- **Business calendars live in Core** (`core_business_calendars`), because OLAs and continuity plans will need them too:
  - weekly hours per ISO weekday in a time zone;
  - extra days off;
  - Romanian public holidays, computed per year (Orthodox Easter and Pentecost, Good Friday since 2018, 6–7 January since 2024).
  - Each organization has at most one default calendar. Without one, Monday–Friday 09:00–17:00, Europe/Bucharest, with Romanian holidays applies.
- **`BusinessTimeCalculator` is pure and exhaustively unit-tested.** Intervals are built in local time and compared as absolute instants, and minutes are added on timestamps, so daylight saving changes do not shift deadlines.
- **Targets per priority** (owner's decision), in working minutes. The ITIL defaults are in code:
  - critical: 15 min response / 4 h resolution;
  - high: 1 h / 8 h;
  - medium: 4 h / 3 days;
  - low: 8 h / 5 days.
  - Organizations override single priorities in `ticketing_sla_targets`.
- **One clock per ticket and metric** (`ticketing_sla_timers`, response and resolution). A clock stores the working minutes consumed before its current run. While it runs, `due_at` and `warn_at` (75 %) are derived from the remaining minutes. Consistency rules are CHECK constraints.
- **Status drives the clocks**, from `TicketLifecycle`, the single place where status changes:
  - **Waiting for the requester (`pending`) pauses** (owner's decision).
  - The first public reply from the service desk completes the response clock.
  - Resolution completes both clocks; reopening restarts the resolution clock with the time already used.
  - Cancelling completes the clocks as "not applicable", never as a breach.
  - A priority change on triage retargets the clocks. A breach already recorded stays recorded.
- **`ticketing:sla:check` runs every minute** (one server, no overlap). It records the warning and the breach once each, and notifies the assignee and the queue's team lead, or the whole queue team when nobody owns the ticket. Partial indexes keep the scan limited to running clocks.
- **Instants are stored in the application time zone (UTC).** Eloquent writes timestamps without an offset, so a deadline computed in the calendar's zone must be converted before saving. They are displayed in `APP_DISPLAY_TIMEZONE`.

## Consequences

- Changing targets or calendars affects new and re-prioritized tickets only. Running clocks are not rewritten, so history stays explainable.
- An account notified of a breach learns of it at most one minute late, or as late as the scheduler, which must run (`schedule:run`).
- Tickets created before this change have no clocks. There is no production data yet, so no backfill is provided.
